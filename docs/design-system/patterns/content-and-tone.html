Content and tone

FaithTech Community sounds like a good City Lead: warm, plain and specific. It uses people's names, says what will happen, and says who can see what.

Overview

The people who use it are FaithTech staff, City Leads, Volunteers, Product Engineers, event attendees and partners. Many of them are volunteering their evenings, so every screen should help them finish the task and get back to people. The voice has three traits:

TraitMeansFrom the mocks
WarmGreets people by name, and thanks without fuss"Good afternoon, Grace", "See you Thu 6 Nov, Abby", "Welcome to FaithTech."
PlainShort sentences and everyday words. Says "you", not "the user""It’s not you. We’ve been told and are looking into it."
SpecificNames the person, the thing, the number and the date"Sent to 41 Product Engineers", "The invite link lasts 14 days."

Faith language belongs to the content people write: event names such as "City Team Prayer & Planning", and announcements. The interface itself stays neutral, so it works for every partner and attendee.

When to use

These rules apply to every string in the app, the emails and the public event page. Tone changes with the moment:

MomentToneExample
Welcome and successWarm, brief"You’re going · We emailed you a confirmation with a calendar invite."
Everyday tasksNeutral, direct"Add contact", "Move to Confirmed", "Start sprint"
PrivacyClear, reassuring"Only you can see this contact and your notes."
Destructive actionsCalm, exact"This removes the contact and all 4 of your notes. It can’t be undone."
ErrorsTakes the blame, gives the fix"We couldn’t reach FaithTech. Nothing was changed — try again."
RefusalsRespectful, names who can help"Ask Grace Okonkwo, your City Lead, if someone needs a role added or removed."

Responsive behaviour

  • Write for the phone first. Titles should fit on one line at 360px: "Invite sent", not "Your invitation has been sent successfully".
  • Never write a separate, shorter copy for small screens. Let lines wrap instead.
  • Meta lines can wrap at the middle dots. Put the most important fact first (the date, then the place).

Accessibility

  • Links and buttons make sense out of context: "Add role to Marcus Lee", "Move Kestrel Labs to another stage", "Remove photo 4". Never "Click here".
  • Every image has alt text that describes it: "Grace smiling, in a head-and-shoulders portrait". Profile photos can't be saved without one.
  • Status is written in words (Cancelled, Members only, Unread, Private), not shown only by colour or a line through the text.
  • Dates are written in full words ("Tue 21 Oct"), never as 21/10, which reads differently from country to country. The audit log is the exception: it uses ISO dates (2026-10-21 10:05) in .mono so the rows line up.
  • Aim for a reading age of about 12. Explain any jargon the first time it appears: "Initiatives hold epics, epics hold stories, stories hold tasks."

Mechanics

  • Sentence case for headings, buttons and labels: "New announcement", "Invite someone". Proper nouns keep their capitals: Planning Poker, Builders Night, Toronto team.
  • Dates: "Tue 21 Oct", "Thu 6 Nov · 6:30 pm". For ranges, use an en dash: "14–15 Nov", "Mon 20 – Fri 31 Oct". Add the year only when it isn't this year.
  • Numbers are digits: "4 notes", "14 days", "41 recipients".
  • Punctuation: curly quotes and apostrophes (’ “ ”), a real ellipsis (…), and the middle dot (·) between facts. Use an em dash only to join two clauses.
  • Names: full name on first mention and in lists (Grace Okonkwo), first name in emails and on your own screens ("Grace assigned you…").
