Foundation · Content

Content

Warm, plain, community-minded words for staff, leads, volunteers and guests: sentence case, real verbs, specific names and numbers, no blame and no insider jargon.

Overview

FaithTech Community talks to people who give their evenings to build things together: City Leads like Grace Okonkwo running Toronto Builders Night, Staff who need to see everything at a glance, volunteers and product engineers like Samuel Mensah, and guests who only ever see a public event page. The copy sounds like a good host: warm, plain and specific about who and what. It is community-minded without church jargon; “gathering”, “partners” and “builders” are fine, insider or devotional language is not.

Lift copy from the mocks before writing new copy; the microcopy library below is the starting point. The pattern page Content and tone shows these rules applied to whole flows.

Sentence case and punctuation

  • Sentence case everywhere: titles, buttons, labels, menu items, toasts. Only the first word and names are capitalised: “Invite someone”, “Add to calendar (.ics)”, “Toronto Builders Night”.
  • Names keep their capitals: roles (City Lead, Staff, System Admin, Volunteer, Product Engineer, Attendee), product features with a proper name (Planning Poker), events (Hackathon: Tech for Shelters) and organisations (Maple Street Church, Lantern Foundation). Kanban stages are “Lead”, “In funnel”, “Confirmed”.
  • Curly quotes and apostrophes in product copy: ’ “ ”. Quoted titles and search terms go in curly quotes: Archive “Shelter problem briefs for teams”?, No results for “zebra crossing”.
  • Question marks end confirm-dialog titles: “Cancel Toronto Builders Night?”, “Remove Ruth Alvarez from Toronto team?”.
  • No full stop on titles, labels, buttons or single-phrase toasts; full stops on sentences in help, error and toast text.
  • Middle dot separates short facts on one line: “Thu 6 Nov · 6:30 pm · Kestrel Labs”, “Toronto · Member since March 2024”.
  • Ellipsis on a busy button, after the "-ing" form: “Adding partner…”, “Sending invite…”.
  • Contractions are fine and friendlier: “didn’t”, “you’re”, “couldn’t”.
  • “Email” with no hyphen; “sign in” (verb) and “sign-in” (noun); British spelling of “organisation”.

  • 12-hour clock with a space and lower-case “am” or “pm”, always with minutes: “7:00 pm”, not “7pm”.
  • Short day and month names, day before month, no comma between them: “Tue 21 Oct”. No year in the current year except on public event pages.
  • Digits for all numbers, including one to nine: “Fix 1 thing to save”, “3 unread”.
  • Plurals follow the number: “1 thing”, “2 things”.
  • An en dash with spaces for ranges: “6:30 – 9:00 pm”, “Votes differ: 5 to 13” in prose.

Error messages

Formula: what didn’t happen (“We couldn’t …”, “Your RSVP wasn’t saved”), what to do next, and that someone already knows when it is our fault. We own the failure (“We couldn’t reach FaithTech”); a field error tells the person what to enter, never that they were “invalid”.

WhereFormulaExample

Error summary“Fix N things to [verb]”, then a linked list“Fix 2 things to RSVP” · “Fix 3 things to publish”
Failed action (toast, dialog)What wasn’t saved + why + Try again“Your RSVP wasn’t saved” · “We couldn’t reach FaithTech. Try again.”
Failed load“We couldn’t load [thing]” + what to try + reassurance“We couldn’t load the partner pipeline” · “Check your connection and try again. If it keeps happening, we’ve already been told about it.”
PermissionWho this is for + who to ask, by name“Only City Leads manage roles” · “Ask Grace Okonkwo, your City Lead, if someone needs a role added or removed.”
Sign-inName both possibilities, never which one“Email or password is wrong. Check both and try again.”

Usage

  • Use real names: “Grace Okonkwo”, “Samuel Mensah”, “Toronto team”. Never “the user”, “the member” or “the record”.
  • Say who can see something whenever privacy is involved: contacts are private, the partner board is shared, Staff is read-only.
  • Talk about people, events, partners and contacts, not “items”, “entities” or “entries”.
  • Keep a toast to a title plus one sentence; keep a field error to one sentence.
  • Leave out church and tech insider jargon; a guest on a public event page should understand every word.
  • Write the accessible name as carefully as the visible text; read it aloud.

Accessibility

  • Plain language with short sentences; it helps people reading in a second language and on a phone.
  • Link and button text stands alone (2.4.4): “Link his profile”, “See all”, not “Click here”; short visible labels get a fuller accessible name (“Move Maple Street Church to another stage”).
  • Counts in accessible names say what they count: “Notifications, 3 unread”, not “3”.
  • Status is never colour alone: “Unread”, “Cancelled”, “Current stage” are written out.
