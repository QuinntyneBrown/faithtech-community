Profile header

The profile header tells members who someone is in FaithTech — name, roles, city — and gives the viewer exactly the actions they are allowed.

Overview

The profile header opens a member's profile: their featured photo or initials, name, global roles, city and join date, and the actions the viewer may take. Below it sits the photo strip, a grid of up to 10 photos, each with alt text.

  • Use it only on pages/profile. Profiles are visible to signed-in members only.
  • Actions depend on who is looking: the owner gets Edit profile; a City Lead viewing someone else gets Add a role; any member can Message by email.
  • Editing never happens inline: Edit profile navigates to pages/edit-profile, Add a role opens a dialog. Photo management (featured, reorder, alt text) is the picture list, not the strip.

Anatomy

  1. AvatarXL avatar: featured photo, or initials on a colour when there is none. aria-hidden; the name follows.
  2. NameThe page <h1>.
  3. Roles.badge--role per global role, in a .cluster. A profile can hold several; new members have none.
  4. Location linePin icon, city and "Member since", muted.
  5. ActionsButtons the viewer is allowed to use, decided by the server.
  6. BioFree text below the head, inside the same card.

Variants

The head changes with the viewer, not with a modifier class.

Photo strip

Square tiles, auto-filling columns of at least 7rem. Every tile is a saved photo with its alt text; photos upload when the profile is saved, so the strip has no processing placeholder.

Sizes

One size: the avatar is always XL and tiles are square with a 7rem minimum.

States

The head itself is static; its actions are buttons with the usual states, and photos are not interactive on the profile.

PartDefaultHoverFocusActive
Own profile actionEdit profileEdit profileEdit profileEdit profile

Content states

Loading (pages/profile/loading): skeleton avatar, title and text in the same head, aria-busy="true", then four square .skeleton--square tiles in the photo strip, where the photos will appear.

No photos yet

Add up to 10 photos. The one you feature becomes your picture across FaithTech.

Add photos
No photos (pages/profile/empty): an empty state replaces the strip; the bio reads "You haven’t written a bio yet." in muted text.

  • Error (pages/profile/error): the whole head is replaced by an error empty state, "We couldn’t load this profile", with Try again.
  • Forbidden: anonymous visitors never see a profile; they are sent to sign in.

Responsive behaviour

  • .profile-head wraps: on a phone the actions drop under the name; the body keeps a 12rem minimum so the name never squeezes beside the avatar.
  • Role badges wrap in their cluster.
  • The photo strip is repeat(auto-fill, minmax(min(100%, 7rem), 1fr)): two or three columns on a phone, up to eight on a desktop, never horizontal scroll.
  • Images are square (aspect-ratio: 1, object-fit: cover) and carry width and height to avoid layout shift.

Theming

Everything comes from semantic tokens: the card surface, muted text, and the role badge. Avatar initials use the avatar's own colour pairs, which switch per theme.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The name is the page's only <h1>. Photos are a <ul> under an <h2> "Photos", each <img> with the alt text the member wrote (required on upload).

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabMoves through the actions in order (Add a role, then Message by email). Photos are not focus stops.
EnterFollows the action.

Labelling

The avatar is aria-hidden because the name is right beside it. The pin icon is decorative; the city is text. Roles are text in badges, never colour only.

Contrast

Location line on surface
Name on surface

Motion

None.

Content

  • Name as the member wrote it; no titles added.
  • Roles use the product names: City Lead, Staff, Volunteer, Product Engineer, Attendee, and lead titles such as Create Lead.
  • Location line: "Toronto · Member since March 2024".
  • Alt text describes the photo, not the file: "Teams working on laptops at the spring hackathon", not "IMG_2041".
  • Empty bio on your own profile: "You haven’t written a bio yet." Others see nothing.
