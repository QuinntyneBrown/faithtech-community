Progress

A rounded bar that shows how far a known amount has got: Sprint 7 has 6 of 24 points done, a photo is 60% uploaded. It turns green when the whole amount is done. Event check-in uses it as an attendance meter (12 of 34 checked in), and Reports lines several up as a bar chart.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stablerole="progressbar"

Overview

Use the progress bar for a value with a known total: completed points in a sprint, or the upload of one profile photo. There is no indeterminate bar; for waiting, use a skeleton or a busy button.

  • Pair it with a label in a .progress-row ("6 of 24 points") so the number is visible, not only the length.
  • Candidate places, where the mocks currently use text only: the sprint header and sprint history on the backlog ("21 of 23 points"), the close-sprint dialog, and a photo row while it uploads ("Uploading… 60%").
  • In use: the attendance meter on event check-in ("12 of 34") and the bar charts on Reports ("Members by city", "New members by month").
  • Where the mocks show sprint progress as text, text alone ("18 of 26 points") is also correct.

Anatomy

  1. Fill.progress__bar; width from --value (a percentage), accent colour.
  2. Track.progress; 8px (12px with --lg), fully rounded, --color-bg-subtle-hover.
  3. Label.progress-row__label: "6 of 24 points", tabular figures, never wraps.

Variants

Two variants: in progress (accent) and complete (success green).

6 of 24 points
24 of 24 · done
Sprint 7 (Mon 20 – Fri 31 Oct) in progress; Sprint 4, Builders Night relaunch, complete. Values from backlog/sprints.

<div class="progress-row">
  <div class="progress progress--lg progress--complete" role="progressbar"
       aria-valuenow="24" aria-valuemin="0" aria-valuemax="24" aria-label="Sprint 4: 24 of 24 points done">

  </div>
  <span class="progress-row__label">24 of 24 · done</span>
</div>

Sizes

Default (8px) sits in table rows and photo rows. Large (12px) is for the one thing a view is about, such as the active sprint header.

  • Not started shows the empty track and "0 of 24", so the sprint still reads as something to begin.
  • Value changes (a story moved to Done, upload bytes arriving) animate the fill over --duration-slow.
  • Complete adds .progress--complete at 100%; the label adds "· done".

Responsive behaviour

  • The track fills whatever width is left in its row; the label keeps its width and never wraps.
  • On phones, table rows use the short label ("21 of 23"); the sprint header can afford "21 of 23 points".
  • At 8% (2 of 24) the fill is only a few pixels; the label makes the value clear at any width.
  • A bar chart keeps three columns down to 360px. The label column takes the longest label ("Product Engineer"), and the bars shrink.

Theming

The track reads --color-bg-subtle-hover, the fill --color-accent, the complete fill --color-success-solid, and the label --color-fg-muted. The value is a custom property, --value, set on the bar, so the component needs no inline width.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A bar chart is different. Its rows are list items whose visible text ("Toronto", "186") carries the data, and each track is aria-hidden="true" because its length compares a row with the largest one rather than measuring progress. The list takes the chart's heading as its name.

The track is role="progressbar" with aria-valuenow, aria-valuemin="0", aria-valuemax (the total) and an aria-label in words ("Sprint 7: 6 of 24 points done"). WAI-ARIA progressbar, read only. An upload bar sits next to a role="status" note so the percentage is announced politely.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabNot a stop; the bar is read only.
Arrow keys (screen reader)Reads "Sprint 7: 6 of 24 points done, progress bar".

Focus

None.

Labelling

Use the real unit (points, percent of a photo) in aria-label, matching the visible label. The visible label repeats the value; it is fine that a screen reader hears it twice.

Motion

The fill width transitions over --duration-slow; reduced motion zeroes it, so the new value appears at once.

Touch

No targets.

Content

  • Label: "{done} of {total}", optionally with the unit: "18 of 26 points".
  • Complete: "24 of 24 · done".
  • Upload: "Uploading… 60%", with the ellipsis character.
  • aria-label: name the thing, then the value: "Sprint 5: 18 of 26 points done".
  • Attendance: leading label "Checked in", trailing "12 of 34".
  • Bar chart: the category on the left, the bare count on the right ("Toronto … 186"), and a footer that says what is counted and what never is ("Private contacts are never counted.").
