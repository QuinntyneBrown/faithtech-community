Link

Links take people somewhere: "Forgot your password?", "Open board", "View his profile". They are underlined, always, and never change data.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableNative <a href>

Overview

A link goes to another page or another place on the page. If activating it changes data (saves, sends, archives), use a button instead. Links are styled by the bare a selector, so any anchor in running text looks right with no class.

  • Inline links sit inside a sentence: "New to FaithTech? Create an account", and each line of an error summary ("Enter your current password") that jumps to its field.
  • Standalone links stand on their own line, in a card header or in a facts list: "Forgot your password?", "Open board" on the Partner pipeline card, "View his profile" on a linked contact, the epic "Tech for Shelters hackathon" on a work item.

Visited links are not styled separately. Members come back to the same people, events and boards every week; a visited colour would turn most of the app a different colour for no useful reason. :visited keeps the default link colour (inherited from Shelfie).

Links with their own look have their own pages: the breadcrumb, top bar nav links and tab bar in navigation, menu items in menu, and anchors given .btn in button.

Anatomy

  1. TextSays where the link goes; it is the accessible name. --color-fg-link, inherits the surrounding font.
  2. UnderlineAlways on. --border-width-hairline thick, offset 0.15em; thickens to --border-width-thick on hover.

Variants

Both variants use the same styles; they differ only in where they sit and how much the text has to carry.

Sizes

Links take the size of the text around them; there is no size modifier.

States

Hover and focus are forced with data-state; real pages get them from :hover and :focus-visible. Visited and active look the same as default on purpose. Links are never disabled: if a destination is not available to this person (a Volunteer cannot open the workspace), do not render the link.

Responsive behaviour

  • Links wrap with the text around them; a long link breaks between words like any other text.
  • A standalone link in a card header ("Open board") stays on the same row as the heading and drops below it when the card is too narrow (.cluster wraps).
  • On touch screens a standalone link is given room by its own line; inline links rely on the 24px minimum spacing exception for text in a sentence.

Theming

Links read two semantic tokens: --color-fg-link and --color-fg-link-hover. In light they are deep violets on a pale page; in dark they switch to --palette-violet-300 so they stay readable on the dark surfaces. Underline thickness comes from the border-width tokens, so a theme never needs to touch it.

a { color: var(--color-fg-link); text-decoration-thickness: var(--border-width-hairline); }
a:hover { color: var(--color-fg-link-hover); text-decoration-thickness: var(--border-width-thick); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Native <a href>. Never an anchor without href, never a <span> with a click handler, never href="#" to run script. Error-summary links point at the field's id (href="#st-current") and move focus there.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves focus to and from the link.
EnterFollows the link. Space does not; it scrolls the page.

Focus

The shared focus ring on :focus-visible, with a small radius so it hugs the text.

Labelling

The link text is the accessible name and must say where it goes out of context: "View his profile", not "here". Where several links share text in a list, add the object in a .visually-hidden span: "Open Toronto Builders Night".

Contrast

Link on page
Link on surface
Link hover on surface
Focus ring on surface

The underline, not colour alone, tells a link from body text (WCAG 1.4.1), so the link-to-text ratio is not required to reach 3:1.

Motion

None. The hover change is instant.

Touch

Standalone links sit on their own line so the whole line height is the target. Inline links fall under the inline exception of WCAG 2.5.8.

Content

  • Say where it goes: "Forgot your password?", "Open board", "View his profile", "Preview public page".
  • Use the name of the thing when the link opens it: "Tech for Shelters hackathon", "Maple Street Church".
  • Error-summary links say what to fix, in the same words as the field error: "Enter a title", "End must be after start".
  • Sentence case, no trailing full stop on a standalone link, no "click here".
