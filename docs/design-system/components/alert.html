Alert and banner

An alert is a tinted box that stays where it is until the situation changes: “That didn’t work”, “Read-only for Staff”. A banner is the same component stretched across the top of every page for news about the member’s account or session.

Overview

Use an alert for a message about one form, dialog or page that must stay visible while the member deals with it. Use a banner (.alert.banner) for a message about their account or session that follows them from page to page. For news about something that just happened and needs no action, use a toast; for a problem with one field, use an inline message.

  • Info: neutral context. "You’re viewing as Staff", "Tomás Ortega just joined FaithTech".
  • Success: something good to see once. "Profile saved", "Your email is verified".
  • Warning: things work, with a catch. "Verify your email to RSVP", "This event was cancelled", "A workspace needs a new owner".
  • Danger: something failed or is blocked. "That didn’t work", "This account is suspended", and the form summary "Fix 3 things to publish".

Anatomy

  1. Status icon20px, --alert-icon, aria-hidden.
  2. Title.alert__title: the message in one line.
  3. Text (optional).alert__text: what it means, what to do.
  4. Actions (optional).alert__actions: one or two small buttons or links ("Review", "Reassign owner", "Send again").
  5. Dismiss (optional).close, 24px, for messages that are only news.
  6. ContainerStatus tint, hairline border with a 4px leading edge, --radius-md, 16px padding.

Sizes

One size. Alerts take the width of their container; banners take the width of the screen.

  • Dismissible: only alerts that are pure news ("Your email is verified", "Tomás Ortega just joined") get a close button. Alerts about a problem or a restriction go away when it does.
  • Persistent: the Staff banner shows on every page of a Staff session; the “No connection” warning banner shows on every page, the offline page included, while the browser is offline and clears when it reconnects; the verify-email banner stays until the address is verified; the suspended banner stays for the whole session.

Responsive behaviour

  • Alerts are fluid; text wraps and actions wrap below the text.
  • Banners below 640px stack title, text and actions in a column, left aligned. From 640px they centre and put them on one row that wraps if needed.
  • Banners push the page down; they never overlap content or the top bar.

Theming

Each status sets four component tokens, --alert-bg, --alert-fg, --alert-border and --alert-icon, from the semantic --color-{status}-bg/-fg/-border/-icon. Themes change the semantic tokens; in the dark theme the tints turn deep and the text light. The dismiss hover is currentColor mixed at 8%, so it follows the status.

.alert--warning { --alert-bg: var(--color-warning-bg); --alert-fg: var(--color-warning-fg);
                  --alert-border: var(--color-warning-border); --alert-icon: var(--color-warning-icon); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

WAI-ARIA status and alert roles. Info, success and warning use role="status" (polite). Danger uses role="alert" (assertive). A form summary adds tabindex="-1" and receives focus on submit. Render the alert into the page when the event happens; an alert already in the HTML at load is read in page order, not announced.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReaches the action, then Dismiss, in reading order.
Enter, SpaceFollows the link, runs the button, or dismisses.
Enter on a summary linkMoves focus to the field with the error.

Focus

Alerts do not move focus, except the form summary, which takes focus after a failed submit. After Dismiss, focus moves to the next focusable element after where the alert was.

Labelling

The icon is aria-hidden; status is said by the title. Dismiss is named "Dismiss". Actions say what they do: "Reassign owner", "Cancel my RSVP", "Send again".

The border is decorative; status is carried by icon, title and tint together.

Motion

None. Alerts appear in place.

Touch

Dismiss is a 24px target pulled into the corner; small action buttons are 32px tall inside a full-width row.

Content

  • Title says what happened, plainly: "We couldn’t save your RSVP", not "Error 503".
  • Text says what was kept and what to do: "Nothing was changed — try again."
  • Restrictions say what the member can still do: "You can see everything but can’t make changes."
  • Form summary title: "Fix N things to <verb>" ("Fix 1 thing to save", "Fix 3 things to publish"); each item is the field’s error, linked.
  • Action labels are verbs: "Review", "Reassign owner", "Send again".
