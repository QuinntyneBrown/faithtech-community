Button

Buttons let Grace and her team do something: publish an event, send an invite, save a contact, cancel an event. One primary button per view says what matters most.

Overview

Use a button when activating it changes something: it submits a form, opens a dialog, copies the event link, shows or hides a password, or archives a contact. Use a link when it only takes the person somewhere. Use the menu for a short list of account actions.

  • Primary is the one action the view exists for: "Save profile", "Publish event", "Send invite", "Start sprint". Never two per view.
  • Secondary (the default look) is for the other actions that still matter: "Cancel" in a dialog footer, "Clear search", "Show all cities", "Review".
  • Ghost is the quiet way out or a row action: "Cancel" under a page form, "Skip for now", "Remove" on a photo, and every icon-only close, copy and show-password button.
  • Danger removes, ends or blocks something: "Cancel event", "Delete permanently", "Suspend", "Remove role", "Archive workspace". It appears in danger zones and in the confirmation dialogs that follow.

A .btn class on an <a href> is fine when the action is really navigation: the ghost "Cancel" under the event form goes back to Events, and the danger "Archive workspace" in a danger zone opens its confirmation dialog. The element stays a link; only the look changes.

Anatomy

  1. Leading icon (optional)1.25em square, 2px stroke, inherits the label colour, aria-hidden="true".
  2. LabelA short verb phrase in sentence case; it is the accessible name.
  3. ContainerHeight from --control-height-*, --radius-full corners (a pill), hairline border, padding from the spacing scale.

Variants

Variants change emphasis, not meaning. Pick by how much attention the action deserves on this view.

Primary, secondary, ghost, danger: highest to lowest emphasis, with danger kept for cancelling, deleting, suspending and archiving.
HTML
<button class="btn btn--primary" type="button">Publish event</button>
<button class="btn" type="button">Cancel</button>
<button class="btn btn--ghost" type="button">Skip for now</button>
<button class="btn btn--danger" type="button">Cancel event</button>

With icons, icon-only and block

Icon-only buttons carry an aria-label; the icon is aria-hidden. "Copy link" and "Show password" sit inside an input group. Block buttons fill the auth card ("Sign in", "Create account", "Send reset link", "Accept and join").
HTML
<button class="btn btn--ghost btn--icon" type="button" aria-label="Close">
  <svg class="icon" aria-hidden="true" viewBox="0 0 24 24">…</svg>
</button>
<button class="btn btn--primary btn--lg btn--block" type="submit">Sign in</button>

Outline

variant="outline" (Angular) is a pill with an accent border and accent text on the surface, filling with --color-accent-subtle on hover. It is the person card's full-width View profile link (Person card); use primary for the one main action of a page.

Sizes

Medium is the default. Small lives in card headers, list rows and the top bar ("Add note", "Comment", "Sign in" on the public event page, the row edit icons). Large is for the one action a whole screen leads to: "Sign in", "Create account", "I’m going" on an event.

Small 36px, medium 44px, large 52px. Heights --control-height-sm/md/lg; padding --space-3/4/6; font --font-size-sm/md/lg.

Both themes

  • Loading keeps the button's width, hides the label behind a spinner and ignores clicks. The spinner is drawn by .btn[aria-busy="true"]::after; put nothing else inside the button (no dot or icon), only its label. One rule for every busy form and dialog: the pressed button takes aria-busy="true" and aria-disabled="true", always both (aria-busy draws the spinner and says work is under way; aria-disabled says it can't be pressed while keeping focus on it, which native disabled would drop). Every other button in a busy dialog (Cancel and Close) takes native disabled, because focus is not on it and it should leave the tab order. A page form's ghost Cancel link stays a working link: leaving the page is always allowed. Nothing is sent twice. Focus stays on the pressed button; the page then moves focus to the result: the toast, the error summary or the next page.
  • Disabled uses the native attribute: not focusable, 50% opacity. It is used for the other buttons in a form or dialog while a submit is in flight, and for nothing else.
  • Pressed applies only to the show-password button in an input group, which sets aria-pressed; toggle choices are switches and chips.

Responsive behaviour

  • On the auth screens (sign in, sign up, forgot and reset password, accept invite) the primary is .btn--lg.btn--block at every width; the card itself is capped at 26rem.
  • In form actions and dialog footers buttons sit end-aligned on one row and wrap when the row is too narrow.
  • One button order everywhere: the way out (Cancel) first, the primary or danger action last, end-aligned. Page forms (.form-actions) and dialog footers (.dialog__footer) follow the same order, so the main action is always in the same place and last in the tab order.
  • Under 640px dialogs become bottom sheets; their footer buttons keep the same order.
  • Every size is at least 36px tall and medium is 44px, so no button needs extra hit area on touch screens.
  • Labels never truncate (white-space: nowrap). A label that does not fit at 360px is too long; shorten the verb phrase.

Theming

The button reads semantic tokens through component tokens: --btn-bg, --btn-fg, --btn-border, --btn-bg-hover, --btn-bg-active, --btn-height, --btn-padding and --btn-font-size. Variants and sizes only reassign those; a context can do the same without touching .btn.

/* A context reassigns the component tokens; it never edits .btn */
.some-context .btn { --btn-height: var(--control-height-sm); --btn-font-size: var(--font-size-sm); }

In the dark theme the accent lightens to --palette-violet-300 and the primary label flips to --color-fg-on-accent (--palette-violet-950), so the label stays dark on a light button. Danger does the same with --palette-red-300.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Native <button type="button">, or type="submit" inside a form. A link that looks like a button is still an <a href>. Never a <div> with a click handler. No ARIA role is needed.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves focus to and from the button in DOM order.
Enter, SpaceActivates the button. Space activates on key up, so a mis-press can be cancelled by moving away first.
Enter in a text fieldSubmits the form through its type="submit" button ("Sign in", "Save settings").

Focus

One shared ring (--focus-ring-width of --color-focus-ring, offset by --focus-ring-offset) on :focus-visible only, so taps and clicks do not show it. The offset gap shows the surface colour, which keeps the ring visible against the primary fill. When a dialog opens, focus starts on its primary action when that is safe ("Add role"); in destructive dialogs it starts on the DELETE or reason field (delete contact, suspend user, cancel event), or on Cancel when there is no field (delete photo, remove role, cancel RSVP), never on the danger button.

Labelling

The visible label is the accessible name. Icon-only buttons set aria-label with the verb and, where several share a view, the object: "Close", "Copy link", "Show password", "Remove photo 3", "Edit note". A loading button keeps its name.

Motion

Background, border and shadow transition over --duration-fast (120ms); the active state moves down 1px. The loading spinner turns once every --duration-loop/2 (700ms). Under prefers-reduced-motion the tokens zero the durations and loops stop after one pass.

Touch

Small 36px, medium 44px and large 52px all clear the 24px minimum; medium and large meet the 44px comfortable target. Small buttons sit at least --space-2 apart in rows.

Content

  • Labels are verb phrases in sentence case: "Save profile", "Send invite", "Add partner", "Move to Confirmed". Never "OK", "Yes" or "Submit".
  • Name the count when it matters: "Send to 41 people" tells Hannah who will get the announcement before she sends it.
  • Destructive labels name what happens: "Cancel event", "Delete permanently", "Remove member". The safe choice is a plain "Cancel".
  • When "Cancel" is also the destructive verb (cancel event, cancel RSVP), the danger button says the full phrase and the safe button names what it keeps, so the two never read the same: "Keep event" beside "Cancel event", "Keep my RSVP" beside "Cancel my RSVP".
  • Keep labels to about three words. The loading label is the "-ing" form of the same verb plus an ellipsis ("Sending invite…", "Saving…"); status text goes in the toast or summary that follows.
