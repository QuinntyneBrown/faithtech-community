Form field

Label, control, help and error, stacked in one block. Every input, textarea and select in FaithTech Community sits in a form field.

Overview

A form field wraps one control with its label and, when needed, a help line and an error line. Every text field, textarea and select in FaithTech Community sits inside one. Groups of radios or checkboxes use a fieldset with a legend.field__label instead.

  • Label is always visible, above the control, and names the value: "Email", "Venue address", "Message to attendees".
  • Optional fields say so in the label: "Phone (optional)". Required fields carry no marker; most fields are required.
  • Hidden label: a lone search box under a page heading keeps its label for assistive technology but hides it visually (.visually-hidden; in code, ft-form-field's labelHidden). Every other field shows its label.
  • Help says something the person needs before they type: "At least 12 characters.", "Leave empty for no limit."
  • Error replaces nothing; it is added under the control and says how to fix the problem.

Anatomy

  1. Labellabel.field__label, --text-label, tied to the control by for.
  2. ControlAn .input, .textarea or .select.
  3. Help or error.field__help in --color-fg-muted, or .field__error in --color-danger-fg with a warning sign; joined to the control with aria-describedby.

The parts stack with a --space-1 gap.

The field's structure is the same for every control; what changes is the control and which optional parts it shows.

Sizes

Not applicable. The field takes its control's size.

  • Invalid sets aria-invalid="true" on the control, adds .field__error and points aria-describedby at it. If the field also has help, the help stays and the error is added below it, and aria-describedby lists both (sign up: "At least 12 characters." then "Use at least 12 characters. A short phrase is easiest to remember.").
  • Read-only is used once: the invite email on Accept invite, which the person cannot change.
  • Disabled is deliberately absent from the mocks: a field the person cannot use is left out, and while a form submits its fields stay editable. The state exists so a control disabled through Angular forms still reads as unavailable (--color-bg-subtle, --color-fg-disabled).
  • Errors appear on submit, never while typing. A form with errors also shows the error summary.

Responsive behaviour

  • Fields are full width of their form, and forms are capped (44rem in Settings, 48rem on the event form).
  • Two short fields can share a row in a .grid ("Starts" and "Ends"); under 640px they stack.
  • Label, help and error wrap; they never truncate.

Theming

The field reads --color-fg-default (label), --color-fg-subtle (optional marker), --color-fg-muted (help) and --color-danger-fg (error). All four are re-mapped by the dark theme; the field itself has no component tokens. The gap between parts is --space-1.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A native <label for> and control. Groups use <fieldset> and <legend>. Help and error are plain text referenced by aria-describedby, so they are read after the label. Required fields have no visible marker, so the control states it: the mocks use native required; in Angular, <ft-form-field required> puts aria-required="true" on its control, and <ft-radio-group required> on its radiogroup. Validation stays with the page, so no browser bubble blocks the inline errors. Optional fields are never marked required.

Announcing errors

An error must be heard without moving focus. In Angular, ft-form-field and ft-radio-group keep a polite live region (aria-live="polite") under the control, rendered before any error, and put the error in it, so it is read when it appears. A clear or a new error updates the same region. A page form with an error summary uses ftFormErrorFocus, which moves focus to the summary or the first invalid field; its fields then skip the live region, so the error is read once, by focus.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabMoves to the control. The label, help and error are not focusable.
Click or tap the labelFocuses the control (or toggles a choice).

Focus

The focus ring is drawn by the control. After a failed submit, focus moves to the error summary, whose links move focus to each field.

Labelling

Every control has a visible label; placeholders are never labels. "(optional)" is part of the label text, so it is announced. Every legend is visible and uses .field__label, even under a section heading: the event form shows "Visibility" below "Who can see and RSVP".

Motion

None. Errors appear without animation.

Touch

The label is part of the target, so tapping "Email" focuses the email field.

Content

  • Labels are nouns in sentence case, no colon: "Venue address", "Message to attendees", "First note".
  • Help is one short sentence ending in a full stop: "If they ever sign up with this email, we’ll let you know."
  • Errors say how to fix it, not what went wrong: "Enter a venue address, or choose Online.", "The end time must be after the start time."
  • The error-summary link uses the short form of the same error, with the same verbs and option names: "Enter a venue or choose Online".
